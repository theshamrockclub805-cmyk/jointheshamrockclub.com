Segment Card: “Why I’m In”

Four questions, every board member, same every time. One sitting → four clips.

PILLAR 6
Behind the Scenes
Q4 doubles as Pillar 2

Setup

Time20 minutes per person. Batch the whole board in one two-hour session — same room, same light, same chair.
GearPhone on a tripod at chest height, vertical 9:16. Lapel mic, always — it is the difference between usable and binned. Window or light behind the camera, never behind them.
FramingSame background for everyone, so twelve clips read as one series. Head and shoulders, eyes a third from the top.
Brief themTopic only, never the exact wording. A rehearsed board member is a worse board member. Tell them: full sentences, under 30 seconds each.

The four questions — ask these, in this order, every time

1. “Tell me who you are and what you do when you’re not doing this.”
Gives you the lower third and makes them a person rather than a title. Cap it at ten seconds. If they start reciting a CV, stop and ask again.
2. “What made you say yes to this?”
Not “why did you join the board” — that gets a press release. This gets a moment. If the first answer is vague, follow up once with “what was happening right before that?” The second pass is usually the real story.
3. “Finish this sentence: a kid in Oxnard shouldn’t have to ______.”
The emotional one, and the hardest to answer in corporate language. Sentence-completion forces something specific where an open question invites waffle. This is the clip that gets shared. Swap the town to match the school you are near.
4. “What would you say to a business owner who’s on the fence?”
Your sales close, in their words — more persuasive from a board member than from us. This is also the clip to send privately to a warm prospect who has not signed yet.

Only time for two? Use 2 and 4. One gives you heart, one gives you the sale.

The rule that makes or breaks this. Before you roll, ban these words out loud, as a joke — “a dollar in the jar every time you say passionate”:
passionate · community impact · give back · make a difference · synergy · excited to announce
Board members default to abstraction, and abstraction is unwatchable. Take the stock phrases away and they have to reach for a real example. The real example is the content.

Rotate one in, so twelve clips differ

  • “What’s the one thing people get wrong about what we do?”
  • “Which school are you secretly rooting for?”
  • “What local business do you love that should be in this?”
  • “What should this look like in three years?”
  • “What’s the smallest thing we’ve done that mattered more than you expected?”
  • “Talk me out of joining.” — usually the most honest answer of the day

On camera, every time

  • Full sentences containing the question. “I said yes because…” The question appears as on-screen text, nobody hears us ask it, so the answer must stand alone.
  • Under 30 seconds per answer. Say the limit before rolling.
  • Roll before and after. The usable take is very often what they say once they think you have stopped.
  • Two takes maximum. Past that they get stiffer, not better.

On-screen treatment (same layover system as everything else)

HookTop line, under 8 words — use the question itself: “What made you say yes?”
Lower thirdName, and role in their own words from Q1 — “Maria · runs a salon on Saviers” beats “Board Member”
Pillar tagCorner label: IN THE FIELD. For Q4 clips use BUSINESS WIN
End cardLast 2 seconds. Logo + “One business per category, per school.” + jointheshamrockclub.com
FontsOswald for the hook and tag, DM Sans for the lower third. Both free, both already in Canva and CapCut

After the shoot

Same day: raw to Dropbox as YYYY-MM-DD_Board_Name_WhyImIn; editor moves finished cuts to /Approved before anything posts. · Turnaround 48 hours — a clip landing three weeks later has missed its moment. · Do not dump all four at once; one per board member per week keeps the feed fed for months.

What one afternoon buys you. 20 minutes × 7 board members = two hours of shooting and 28 finished clips, most of a quarter of content. The batching argument from the marketing plan, applied to people instead of places: the setup costs the same for one person or seven, so film seven.
