Oxnard USA Rewards — Four Decisions

The missing piece of the three-way win. Nothing about rewards can be promoted until these are answered.

Why this is the first thing to settle. The rewards loop is what separates us from an ordinary banner sponsorship, and it is the only part of the story we cannot finish a sentence about. It blocks a content pillar, four segments, every consumer call to action, and the best line in the sales pitch: “your customers get something too.”

Where the gap is

The four questions

1. What does the shopper actually receive?

A. A percentage off — simplest to explain, comes off the business’s margin · B. Points or stamps across sponsors — drives repeat visits, needs tracking · C. A perk rather than a discount (free add-on, upgrade) — feels generous, costs the business far less · D. Entry to a prize draw — cheapest, weak per visit · E. Nothing material, just knowing a school benefits — free, and the weakest

2. How do they claim it?

A. Show the Oxnard USA app at the register · B. Scan a QR code on the window decal · C. Say a code word · D. Automatic via linked card — best experience, most expensive to build
The test to apply: anything that needs a part-time cashier to remember something will work perhaps half the time. Whatever we pick has to survive a busy Saturday with a new employee on the till.
Decision: ______________________________________________________________________

3. Who pays for the reward?

A. The business absorbs it · B. Shamrock funds it from sponsorship revenue · C. Oxnard USA funds it · D. Shared
If the answer is A, it must be said at the point of sale. A sponsor who pays $997 and then discovers they are also expected to discount every rewards customer will feel ambushed, and will say so to other businesses. If the business bears a cost, it belongs on the sponsorship page and in the agreement, not in the small print of an onboarding email.
Decision: ______________________________________________________________________

4. How does the school benefit from a purchase?

A. A share of each transaction goes to the school — the strongest story, and the hardest: it needs real payment plumbing, reconciliation, and care about what a 501(c)(3) claims about where money goes · B. Milestone-based — “500 redemptions this term bought the band new stands” · C. No per-purchase link. The school benefit is the sponsorship fee; rewards exist to make that sponsorship worth more to the business
Worth saying plainly: C is the honest default and is easier to defend than it sounds. Rewards drive footfall → the sponsorship pays for itself → the business renews → the school keeps being funded. Do not claim A until the plumbing exists; a nonprofit saying a purchase funds a school needs to be able to show that it did.

What this unblocks, the day it is answered

Content pillar 5 (about one post a week that currently cannot be made) · four playbook segments — Shamrock Finds (20), Redeem With Me (21), Mystery Shopper (13), UGC Challenge (23) · the consumer CTA “shop Shamrock businesses and get rewarded” · the strongest line in the sales pitch, that a sponsor’s own customers get something too · and the window decal artwork, which cannot be finalised until we know what it tells people to do.

Until it is answered

Do not promote a rewards program in any post, flyer or pitch. A shopper who turns up expecting a reward that does not exist is a bad experience for them, for the business and for us. Keep selling on exclusivity and visibility, which are real today. — First thing to film once decided: Redeem With Me, one real person start to finish, thirty seconds. People believe a demonstration sooner than a diagram.
